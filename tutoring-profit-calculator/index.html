How to work out what your tutoring business really earns

Most tutoring business owners can tell you their monthly revenue within a few hundred. Very few can tell you their profit. The gap between those two numbers is where almost every struggling tutoring business is actually losing.

There are four things standing between a booked session and money in your account, and this calculator walks through all of them in order.

1. Sessions you schedule vs. sessions you get paid for

Every tutoring business has a no-show and late-cancellation rate. A rate of 5–10% is normal; above 15% usually means your cancellation policy either doesn't exist or isn't enforced. The cost is invisible because it never appears on an invoice — you simply bill less than you planned, and the shortfall gets blamed on "a slow month".

The number that changes people's minds is the annual one. A business running 180 sessions a month at $60 with an 8% no-show rate is losing about $860 a month — over $10,000 a year — to sessions that were on the calendar and never got billed.

2. Whether you still pay tutors for no-shows

Many owners pay tutors for a booked slot regardless of attendance. It's fair, and it keeps good tutors. But if the family isn't charged and the tutor is paid, that session has a 100% negative margin. Tick that box in the calculator and watch what happens to your profit per student. This single policy decision is often worth more than a price increase.

3. Invoices that are billed but never collected

This is the one owners consistently underestimate. Invoiced revenue feels like earned revenue, so it gets counted mentally and then quietly written off months later. In a business with 45 students, a 6% unpaid rate is roughly $600 a month sitting in someone else's bank account.

Unpaid invoices almost never fail because families can't pay. They fail because nobody followed up on day eight, and by day sixty the conversation has become awkward. Collection is a scheduling problem, not a money problem.

4. What's left after tutor pay and fixed costs

A healthy tutoring business keeps somewhere between 15% and 30% net margin after tutor pay, fixed costs and the owner's own salary. Below 10%, you own a job with extra admin. Above 35%, you're usually either underpaying tutors or teaching most sessions yourself, and neither scales.

How much should I charge for tutoring?

Work backwards instead of copying local competitors. Decide the tutor share you need to attract good people — commonly 50–60% of the session fee — add your fixed cost per session, then add your target margin. Test the rate here before you announce it. A $5 increase across 180 sessions a month is $10,800 a year, and it rarely costs you a single student.

What counts as a fixed cost?

  • Rent or room hire, utilities, insurance
  • Scheduling, accounting and payment software
  • Advertising, website, directory listing fees
  • Materials, assessments, licences
  • Your own salary — if you don't pay yourself, your margin is fiction

Frequently asked questions

Is this calculator free?

Yes, completely, with no sign-up. Everything runs in your browser and none of your numbers are sent anywhere or stored.

What if I charge monthly tuition instead of per session?

Divide your monthly tuition by the number of sessions included to get an effective per-session rate, then enter that. The maths works out identically.

What's a normal no-show rate for tutoring?

Between 5% and 10% for most businesses with a written cancellation policy. Businesses without an enforced policy commonly run 12–20%.

How do I actually reduce unpaid invoices?

Three things, in this order: a written payment policy signed at enrolment, an invoice due date that isn't "whenever", and a follow-up you send on a fixed schedule rather than when you happen to remember. The hard part is the third one, and it's the reason most owners eventually track it in a system instead of in their head.
